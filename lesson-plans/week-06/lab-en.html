Lab 05 — Overfitting Reproduction and Regularization: six arms, five seeds

1. Learning Objectives

Knowledge

  • State the six fixed items of the experiment protocol and explain why each one is fixed
  • Explain how a training set of 80 samples against 17,226 parameters produces overfitting on purpose, and why that is the point of the lab
  • State the mechanism of each brake: Early Stop, Weight Decay, Dropout, Data Augmentation
  • Explain why Validation Loss turning upward is the evidence of overfitting, and why validation accuracy is not
  • Explain why a difference smaller than one standard deviation cannot be reported as a result
  • Explain why the two arms that lost test accuracy are underfitting rather than failed regularization

Skills

  • Run the six-arm comparison on the same fixed protocol, with five seeds per arm (42, 43, 44, 45, 46)
  • Implement early stopping with a patience counter and log the best epoch and the best validation loss
  • Quantify generalization with one number: the rise from the best validation loss to the final one
  • Plot training and validation curves per arm and read overfitting or underfitting off the shape
  • Write a verdict with a number, a comparison and a mechanism — including the sentence "no significant difference was observed"
  • Ask an AI for overfitting causes, then verify every cause against the curves and delete the ones the curves do not support
Attitudes Reporting an unsuccessful experiment honestly is a basic requirement of research integrity. Two of the six arms in this lab make the model worse; they stay in the report and they get explained. The rule for the whole lab: one variable per arm, every arm measured over five seeds, and no sentence in the conclusion that the curves do not support.

4. Key Activity Design

Activity 1 · Write the Prediction Before the Protocol (Lab A, p22–p23)

Before any code runs, every student writes one prediction: which of the six arms will end with the lowest test accuracy, and why. The room usually predicts augmentation or the combined arm — the two that do lose. Writing it down first converts the rest of the lab from "running a script" into "checking a claim", and it makes the honest reporting of the failed arms feel like a result rather than a confession.

Activity 2 · Find the Epoch Where It Starts to Overfit (Lab B, p23)

Each student must write three numbers into the log for the baseline arm: the best validation loss (0.516), the epoch it occurred at (85 ± 17), and the validation loss at epoch 400 (0.671). Every other claim in the report has to be consistent with those three numbers. Students who cannot find the minimum have almost always plotted the training loss instead of the validation loss, and the correction is quick and permanent.

Activity 3 · The Arm Where Training Accuracy Falls (Lab C, p20)

The Data Augmentation arm is the only one whose training accuracy is below 1.000: 0.960 ± 0.027. Students who were told "regularization improves generalization" read that number as noise. The instructor asks them to go one step further and check the test accuracy: 0.824 against the baseline's 0.878. The conclusion they must write is that on 64-pixel images, a ±1 pixel shift and σ = 0.15 noise erase the stroke structure — the augmented copies are close to wrong labels, so the model is now underfitting.

Activity 4 · Two Numbers and One Standard Deviation (Lab D, p21)

Put the Dropout test accuracy 0.884 ± 0.011 next to the baseline 0.878 ± 0.012 and require the class to decide, on the board, whether Dropout is better. The gap is 0.006; the standard deviation is 0.012. The class computes both, sees that one run cannot settle it, and writes the sentence that most students have never written before: no significant difference was observed. This is the single most transferable habit in the lab.

Activity 5 · AI Diagnosis, Then Curve Verification (Lab E, p24)

Students ask an AI why their baseline model overfits; the AI typically returns a confident list of causes, including ones that the measured curves contradict. Each proposed cause must be checked against the train and validation curves and either kept with its evidence or deleted. The deliverable is the shortened list plus one line of curve evidence per surviving cause. Deleting an AI explanation on the basis of your own measurement is the skill being assessed.

How to Read the Measured Arms · Three Honest Conclusions 1. Regularization suppresses the rise of validation loss; it does not raise accuracy. All six arms land between 0.716 and 0.858 in validation accuracy, and every test-accuracy difference sits inside one standard deviation (0.010–0.024) — accuracy cannot tell the arms apart, validation loss can. 2. Data Augmentation and the combined arm are harmful on 8×8 images because they cause underfitting, not because regularization failed. 3. A single run cannot settle a comparison: the same change moves by up to ±3 percentage points across seeds, and Dropout 0.884 against baseline 0.878 is a gap of 0.006 — smaller than the 0.012 standard deviation.
Before Students Leave Confirm four things for every student: the notebook runs top-to-bottom; w6_results.json holds all six arms with five seed rows each; the three required baseline numbers (0.516, 85 ± 17, 0.671) appear in the log with a curve next to them; and the conclusion contains at least one sentence about an arm that did not help. An arm that failed is still an arm that was measured.
