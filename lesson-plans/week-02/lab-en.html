4. Key Activity Design

Activity 1 · Data Health Check Station (Lab B, p26)

In 3 minutes, students use info() + describe() + isna().sum() to "health-check" the Titanic dataset, then write down "3 most surprising findings." The instructor collects 2-3 student findings, e.g. "the Cabin column is 77% missing — should we drop it?" — setting up the cleaning discussion.

Activity 2 · Hypothesis Generation & Verification (Lab C, p27)

Building on the three charts, students propose at least 3 hypotheses (e.g. "third-class survival rate is lowest," "higher fare means higher survival," "children survived more than adults") and write them in the notebook's Markdown. This trains the mindset of "answering questions with data" — observe first, then form hypotheses, rather than starting with a conclusion and finding data to match.

Activity 3 · Cleaning Strategy Quick Debate (Lab D, p28)

For the Age column missing values (177/891 = 20%), the instructor runs a quick hand poll: drop vs median-fill vs fill-by-class? Two or three students with different choices each give a one-sentence rationale; the instructor summarizes "there is no single right answer, but there must be reasoning — and a record of it." This leads into the correct "split before imputing" workflow for formal ML projects.

Activity 4 · Feature Engineering Challenge (optional bonus, after Lab E)

Challenge: beyond the textbook examples (Title / FamilySize / IsAlone), what new features can you extract from existing columns? The most creative feature (e.g. extracting cabin info from Ticket number patterns, extracting the deck from the Cabin first letter) will be showcased at the start of next week's class. Students with spare capacity complete it after class; it counts as Lab bonus points.
