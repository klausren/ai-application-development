Lesson Plan · Week 3 · Lecture Part (Theory · 90 min)

Data Preparation and Pipelines

⏱ Weekly 180-min integrated session · this plan covers the Lecture Part, 90 min (L1–L5) 📚 Chapter 3.1–3.7 🎯 Module 1 · AI Application Development Foundations and PyTorch 🧩 Lesson Plan ID: LP-W3-A 🌐 English

1. Learning Objectives

Knowledge

  • State the week's thesis — data sets the ceiling, models approach it — and use it to decide what to fix first when results are poor
  • Name the six steps of the pipeline and explain why their order is not negotiable
  • Give an explicit detection criterion and an action for each of the four data problems: missing values, outliers, duplicates, label noise
  • Distinguish random, stratified, time-based and group splitting, and choose one from the nature of the data
  • Define data leakage and identify its four common shapes
  • State the two-method contract of a Dataset and the four jobs of a DataLoader
  • Explain how augmentation and imbalance treatment must be bounded, and which metrics to switch to under imbalance

Skills

  • Read an exploration report and convert every claim into one assert or one plot
  • Run a seeded, stratified split with torch.Generator and report the class ratios of all three splits
  • Implement a custom torch.utils.data.Dataset returning one (x, y) sample
  • Configure a DataLoader and justify batch_size, shuffle, num_workers and generator
  • Detect leakage using one question: is this information available at inference time?
  • Treat imbalance with WeightedRandomSampler or CrossEntropyLoss(weight=...) and re-measure with per-class metrics
Attitudes Internalise that a metric is only as trustworthy as the protocol that produced it. Train the habit of asking could the model have seen this during training? before celebrating any score — and of treating data provenance and privacy as a hard boundary rather than a formality. Respecting the source of your data is the baseline of engineering ethics.

4. Key Activity Design

Activity 1 · Where Is the Ceiling? (L1, p4)

Write a score on the board — "accuracy 0.91" — and ask the class to name five things that could be wrong with it. They will list models first. Push them back to data: coverage, label quality, split, duplication, timestamps. Close with the thesis line: a model can only approach the ceiling the data set; if the ceiling is low, no architecture rescues it.

Activity 2 · Which Bucket? (L1, p6)

Read out eight actions — "switch to a bigger model", "check duplicate keys", "fix the random seed", "tune the learning rate", "clip the 1/99 percentiles", "add more layers", "check the label agreement rate", "write the split ratio into config". Students sort each into model-centric or data-centric. The debrief: the data-centric column is the one you can still act on when the metric refuses to move.

Activity 3 · Write the Assert That Refutes the AI (L2, p10–p11)

Project three AI claims about a dataset and ask students to write, in 60 seconds each, the one line that would prove or disprove it: "classes are balanced, train as is" → assert share.min() > 0.20; "there are no missing values" → assert df.isna().sum().sum() == 0; "features correlate strongly with the label" → plot it and recompute independently. The lesson lands as a rule: every claim needs one assert or one plot, and a disagreement goes into AI_USE.md.

Activity 4 · Legal or Leak? (L3, p15–p16)

Draw the split line on the board. Read operations one at a time — "compute the mean over the whole column and subtract it", "median-fill before splitting", "hyperparameter search on the test set", "shuffle a time series before cutting it", "put all records of one device on the same side of the line" — and ask each time: legal or leak? Ends with the single test that generalises: could you obtain this information at inference time? If not, it must not appear in training.

Activity 5 · The Exam You Already Saw (L3, p15)

Tell the production incident: a team trained on a frame that included the test samples, reported 0.99 offline, and dropped to 0.78 in production. Ask the class where the 21 points went. The answer is the point of the whole week — the model did not learn a rule, it had seen the exam. Then ask them to name the one-line check that would have caught it before the release.
