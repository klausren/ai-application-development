p5数据角色与质量六维度 · Data Role & QualityHero · 6 Dimensions~4 min

Here is the thesis of this whole week: in traditional software, logic is king — you write the rules. In AI software, data is king — the rules are learned from the data. Same input, same algorithm, but better data beats a better algorithm almost every time. So how do you judge data? Six dimensions. Completeness — are values missing? Accuracy — do values match reality? Consistency — does the same fact look the same everywhere, or does age say 20 in one table and 200 in another? Timeliness — is it fresh enough for the question? Relevance — does it actually relate to what you're predicting? And representativeness — the sneakiest one: does your sample look like the real world it will face? Keep these six words in your head — they are your stethoscope for every dataset you'll ever meet.

这是本周的总论点：传统软件里逻辑为王——你写规则；AI 软件里数据为王——规则是从数据里学出来的。同样的输入、同样的算法，更好的数据几乎总能打败更好的算法。那怎么评判数据？六个维度。完整性——有缺失吗？准确性——值与事实相符吗？一致性——同一事实在各处长得一样吗，还是这张表年龄 20、那张表 200？及时性——对这个问题够新吗？相关性——和你要预测的东西真有关系吗？代表性——最阴险的一个：你的样本像它将来要面对的真实世界吗？把这六个词装进脑子——它们是你今后遇到任何数据集时的听诊器。

p6数据类型与来源 · Types & SourcesStructured / Semi / Unstructured~3 min

Data comes in three shapes. Structured data — tables with rows and columns, like a spreadsheet or a database: every value has a fixed place. This is what Pandas eats for breakfast. Semi-structured — JSON files, XML, logs: organized, but flexible — keys can appear or disappear. Unstructured — images, audio, free text, video: no schema at all, and roughly eighty percent of the world's data lives here. And where does data come from? Databases, APIs, files, sensors, web scraping, and human labeling. One warning I'll repeat all semester: your data source decides your quality ceiling. If the source is biased or broken, no model downstream can fix it. Choose sources like you choose ingredients — the chef can't rescue rotten fish.

数据有三种形态。结构化——行列分明的表，像电子表格或数据库，每个值都有固定位置；这是 Pandas 的早餐。半结构化——JSON、XML、日志：有组织但灵活，字段可出现可消失。非结构化——图像、音频、自由文本、视频：完全没有 schema，世界上约 80% 的数据在这里。数据从哪来？数据库、API、文件、传感器、爬虫、人工标注。一个我整学期都会重复的警告：数据来源决定质量上限——来源有偏或损坏，下游任何模型都救不回来。选数据源要像选食材——厨师救不了烂鱼。

p7坏数据案例 · When Data Goes WrongAI Hiring Bias Case~3.5 min

Let me tell you a true story about what bad data costs. Around 2014, one of the world's largest tech companies built an AI to screen job applications — trained on ten years of their own hiring history. Sounded smart. But their historical hires were mostly men, so the model learned a dangerous shortcut: penalize the word "women's" — as in "women's chess club captain" — and downgrade graduates of women's colleges. The system was quietly discriminating, and by 2018 they shut it down entirely. Now map the story onto our six dimensions: the data was complete and accurate — the resumes were real. It was representativeness that failed: the data reflected a biased past, and the model faithfully amplified it. The lesson is not "AI is evil". The lesson is: a model trained on your history will repeat your history — at scale. Quick check, four questions on the slide — shout the dimension: temperatures recorded in Fahrenheit but stored as Celsius? ... Accuracy. A customer table where twenty percent of emails are empty? ... Completeness. Training a face detector only on photos of young people, then deploying it on everyone? ... Representativeness. A stock model trained on data that ends in 2019? ... Timeliness. Good — the stethoscope works.

讲一个关于坏数据代价的真实故事。大约 2014 年，全球最大的科技公司之一做了个 AI 简历初筛系统——用他们自己十年的招聘历史训练。听起来很聪明。但历史录用者大多为男性，于是模型学到一个危险捷径：惩罚"women's"这个词——比如"女子国际象棋社社长"——并压低女子学院毕业生的评分。系统在悄悄歧视，2018 年被彻底关停。把故事对到六维度上：这些数据完整且准确——简历都是真的；失败的是代表性：数据反映了有偏的过去，模型忠实地放大了它。教训不是"AI 是恶的"，教训是：用你的历史训练的模型，会规模化地重演你的历史。快问快答，幻灯片上四道题，齐声喊维度：华氏度当摄氏度存？——准确性。客户表 20% 邮箱为空？——完整性。人脸检测只用年轻人照片训练却要服务所有人？——代表性。股票模型的数据止于 2019 年？——及时性。很好，听诊器好使了。

p9双工具谱系 · NumPy & PandasHero · Division of Labor~3 min

Here's the mental model I want you to keep forever. NumPy is the engine; Pandas is the cockpit. NumPy gives you the ndarray — a grid of numbers, all the same type, stored in one contiguous block of memory, with math that runs at C speed. Pandas builds on top of it: it wraps NumPy arrays with labels — row index and column names — so you can say df["Age"] instead of arr[:, 3]. The rule of thumb: when your data is pure numbers and you're doing math, stay in NumPy. When your data is a real table — mixed types, missing cells, meaningful column names — reach for Pandas. And here's the part most beginners miss: under the hood, every Pandas column is a NumPy array. So when you write df["Price"] * 0.9, Pandas hands the whole column to NumPy and the vectorized math runs there. Two tools, one stack.

这是我要你们永远记住的心智模型：NumPy 是发动机，Pandas 是驾驶舱。NumPy 给你 ndarray——一个数字网格，类型统一、连续内存存放、数学运算以 C 速度执行。Pandas 建在它之上：给 NumPy 数组套上标签——行索引和列名——于是你可以写 df["Age"] 而不是 arr[:, 3]。经验法则：纯数字、纯数学 → 留在 NumPy；真实的表——混合类型、缺失格、有意义的列名 → 用 Pandas。多数初学者漏掉的一点是：底层上，Pandas 的每一列就是一个 NumPy 数组。所以你写 df["Price"] * 0.9 时，Pandas 把整列交给 NumPy，向量化数学在那里跑。两个工具，一条技术栈。

p10向量化 · VectorizationLoop vs Vectorized~4 min

Look at the two code blocks on the slide. Left: the Python loop — create an empty list, iterate ten million times, append, append, append. Right: prices * 1.1. One line. And when we time them, the one-liner is not slightly faster — it's often fifty to a hundred times faster. Why? Three reasons, top to bottom. First: a Python loop re-interprets every operation, every single iteration — type checks, dispatch, object overhead. NumPy calls a pre-compiled C routine once. Second: contiguous memory. A NumPy array is one unbroken block, so the CPU loads it into fast cache and streams through it; a Python list is a bag of pointers scattered across the heap. Third: SIMD — single instruction, multiple data — the CPU can apply one instruction to four or eight numbers at the same cycle. So the habit I'm grading you on from today: whenever you find yourself writing a for-loop over numbers, stop and ask: can NumPy do this whole array at once? Ninety percent of the time, the answer is yes.

看幻灯片上两段代码。左边：Python 循环——建空列表、迭代一千万次、append、append、append。右边：prices * 1.1，一行。计时之后，这行代码不是快一点——常常快 50 到 100 倍。为什么？三条原因，自上而下。第一：Python 循环每次迭代都重新解释操作——类型检查、分发、对象开销；NumPy 只调用一次预编译的 C 例程。第二：连续内存。NumPy 数组是一整块，CPU 可以整块装进高速缓存流式处理；Python 列表是一袋指针，散落堆上各处。第三：SIMD——单指令多数据——CPU 可以一个周期对四到八个数同时执行同一指令。所以从今天起我要考核的习惯是：每当你要对数字写 for 循环，停下来问一句：NumPy 能不能整列一次做完？九成情况下，答案是能。

提示 TIP三原因只需记住关键词：C 层 / 连续内存 / SIMD，Lab A 会在热身任务里摸到
p11索引 · 切片 · 广播Index / Slice / Broadcast~5 min

Three survival skills for NumPy. Indexing and slicing: a[0] first element, a[1:4] elements one to three, and for a 2-D array, a[rows, cols] with a comma — a[:, 0] means all rows, first column. One trap, and it bites everyone at least once: a slice is a view, not a copy. Modify the slice, and you've modified the original array. If you need independence, say so explicitly: a[1:4].copy(). Aggregation: a.sum(), a.mean(), a.max(), a.argmax() — and for 2-D, the axis argument: axis 0 collapses down the rows giving you one number per column, axis 1 collapses across the columns giving you one number per row. My memory trick: axis is the direction the result shrinks along. Broadcasting is the magic that makes shapes play together. Rule: compare shapes from the trailing edge; dimensions are compatible if they're equal, or one of them is 1 — the size-1 dimension stretches. Matrix times vector, matrix plus scalar, column vector plus row vector becomes a full outer sum. Broadcasting is why NumPy code stays short — but always check shapes when something breaks: a.shape is your best debugging friend.

NumPy 三条生存技能。索引与切片：a[0] 第一个元素，a[1:4] 第 1 到第 3 个，二维数组用逗号 a[行, 列]——a[:, 0] 表示所有行、第一列。一个陷阱，人人至少中招一次：切片是视图不是副本——改了切片，原数组也变了。需要独立就明说：a[1:4].copy()。聚合：a.sum()、a.mean()、a.max()、a.argmax()；二维还有 axis 参数：axis 0 沿行向下塌缩，得到每列一个数；axis 1 沿列横向塌缩，得到每行一个数。我的记忆口诀：axis 就是结果收缩的方向。广播是让形状互相配合的魔法。规则：从尾部对齐比较；相等或其中一个为 1 即兼容——长度 1 的维度会被拉伸。矩阵乘向量、矩阵加标量、列向量加行向量变成完整的外和。广播正是 NumPy 代码短的原因——但出问题时永远先查形状：a.shape 是你最好的调试朋友。

p12Pandas 操作链 · WorkflowLoad → Inspect → Select → Transform → Aggregate~4 min

Every Pandas session you will ever run follows the same five-step chain — memorize the verbs. Load: pd.read_csv("titanic.csv") — one line, and your file becomes a DataFrame. Inspect: df.head() first five rows, df.info() — column types and missing counts, this is your first diagnostic, run it every time, no exceptions — and df.describe() for the statistical snapshot. Select: single column df["Age"] gives a Series; double brackets df[["Age", "Fare"]] give a DataFrame; and boolean masks: df[df["Age"] > 30] returns only the rows where the condition is True. Transform: create new columns — df["FamilySize"] = df["SibSp"] + df["Parch"] + 1 — that's feature engineering in embryo, remember this move for Week 3. Aggregate: df.groupby("Pclass")["Fare"].mean() — split the table by class, apply mean to Fare, combine back. That split-apply-combine pattern answers half of all real analysis questions. Five verbs, one chain — load, inspect, select, transform, aggregate. In the Lab today you will run this exact chain, in this exact order, on the Titanic data.

你今后跑的每一次 Pandas 会话都是同一条五步链——把这些动词背下来。加载：pd.read_csv("titanic.csv")——一行，文件变 DataFrame。检查：df.head() 前五行；df.info()——列类型与缺失计数，这是你的第一诊断，每次必跑、没有例外；df.describe() 看统计快照。选取：单列 df["Age"] 得到 Series；双括号 df[["Age", "Fare"]] 得到 DataFrame；布尔掩码：df[df["Age"] > 30] 只返回条件为 True 的行。变换：建新列——df["FamilySize"] = df["SibSp"] + df["Parch"] + 1——这就是特征工程的雏形，第 3 周还会用到。聚合：df.groupby("Pclass")["Fare"].mean()——按舱位切表、对 Fare 求均值、合并回来。split-apply-combine 模式能回答一半的真实分析问题。五个动词、一条链——加载、检查、选取、变换、聚合。今天 Lab 你们就要按这条链、按这个顺序，在 Titanic 数据上跑一遍。

p13实战速查 · Cheat Sheet9 Lines + Chain Indexing Trap~3.5 min

These nine lines on the slide are your cheat sheet — I'd suggest photographing this page right now; you'll use it in the Lab in twenty minutes. Walk through them fast: read_csv, head, info, describe, boolean mask, value_counts — which, by the way, is the fastest way to see category balance, like how many passengers embarked at each port — sort_values, groupby-mean, and dropna. Now the trap at the bottom: chained indexing. df[df["Age"] > 30]["Fare"] — read-only, fine. But df[df["Age"] > 30]["Fare"] = 0? Pandas returns a temporary copy for the first bracket, assigns into that copy, and throws it away — your assignment silently vanishes, and you may get the famous SettingWithCopyWarning. The fix: one shot, with .loc — df.loc[df["Age"] > 30, "Fare"] = 0 — row condition and column target in a single indexing operation. Rule to remember: when you assign, never chain; use .loc and do it in one step.

幻灯片上这九行就是你的速查表——建议现在就拍照，二十分钟后 Lab 就要用。快速过一遍：read_csv、head、info、describe、布尔掩码、value_counts——顺带说，这是看类别均衡最快的方法，比如每个登船港口各有多少人——sort_values、groupby-mean、dropna。然后底部的陷阱：链式索引。df[df["Age"] > 30]["Fare"]——只读，没事。但 df[df["Age"] > 30]["Fare"] = 0 呢？Pandas 为第一层括号返回一个临时副本，赋值进了副本，然后扔掉——你的赋值无声消失，还可能收到著名的 SettingWithCopyWarning。修法：用 .loc 一步到位——df.loc[df["Age"] > 30, "Fare"] = 0——行条件与列目标放在同一次索引操作里。要记住的规则：赋值时永远不要链式；用 .loc 一步完成。

p15四图四问 · Chart SelectionHistogram / Scatter / Bar / Heatmap~4 min

You don't pick a chart because it looks pretty — you pick it because you have a question. Four charts, four questions, matched one-to-one on the slide. Histogram — "how is this value distributed?" One numeric column, binned: is Age centered on thirty, or bimodal with a children's bump? Shape, center, spread, outliers — all visible in one glance. Scatter plot — "do these two numbers relate?" Two numeric columns, one dot per row: is there a line, a curve, a funnel, or just a cloud? Bar chart — "which category is bigger?" One categorical, one numeric: average fare by passenger class — three bars, instant answer. Heatmap — "which pairs correlate?" The whole correlation matrix colored at once — in the Titanic data you'll see a strong negative block between Pclass and Fare: higher class, lower number, higher price. And Matplotlib mechanics in one breath: plt.figure, plt.hist / plt.scatter / plt.bar, always plt.xlabel, plt.ylabel, plt.title — in this course, a chart without axis labels and a title loses points. No exceptions.

选图不是因为好看，而是因为你有问题。四张图、四个问题，幻灯片上一一对应。直方图——"这个数值怎么分布？"单数值列分箱：Age 是集中在 30 岁，还是有儿童凸起的双峰？形状、中心、离散、离群，一眼全见。散点图——"这两个数有关系吗？"两数值列，一行一个点：是直线、曲线、喇叭口，还是一团云？条形图——"哪个类别更大？"一分类型一数值：各舱位平均票价——三根柱子，即刻有答案。热力图——"哪些变量两两相关？"整个相关矩阵一次上色——Titanic 数据里你会看到 Pclass 与 Fare 之间的强负相关块：舱位等级数字越小、价格越高。Matplotlib 机制一口气说完：plt.figure、plt.hist/plt.scatter/plt.bar，永远加 plt.xlabel、plt.ylabel、plt.title——本课程里，没有轴标签和标题的图直接扣分，没有例外。

p16EDA 五步法 · The Five StepsShape → Quality → Distributions → Relations → Questions~5 min

Exploratory Data Analysis is not wandering around your data hoping for inspiration — it's a fixed route with five stations, and this exact sequence is also in your textbook, section 2.5.1, so the numbering there matches this slide. Step 1 — Shape: df.shape. 891 rows, 12 columns. Ten seconds, and you already know the scale of the world you're entering. Step 2 — Quality: df.info() and df.isnull().sum(). Which columns have holes, which types are wrong. This step decides your cleaning plan — never skip it. Step 3 — Distributions: df.describe(), plus histograms for the numeric columns that matter. Where's the center, what's the spread, is anything absurd. Step 4 — Relations: group means for categorical-versus-target, scatter or correlation for numeric pairs. This is where hypotheses are born — does first class really survive more? Step 5 — Questions: write down what surprised you and what you'd check next. EDA ends not with an answer, but with a sharper question. Three lines of code preview on the slide — df.shape, df.info(), df.describe() — that's the skeleton of steps 1 to 3; the Lab will complete all five.

p19清洗六步 · Six-Step PipelineStandard Workflow~3.5 min

Six steps, in order. One — inspect: you've done it; info, isnull-sum, describe — you know your enemy. Two — handle missing values: we'll spend a whole slide on the decision path. Three — handle outliers: detect, then decide — three methods and three dispositions, next slide. Four — fix types and duplicates: a date column read as string? pd.to_datetime. Numeric column with stray text? Coerce and re-inspect. Duplicates: df.duplicated().sum() then df.drop_duplicates(). Five — standardize formats: "M"/"male"/"Male" are three spellings of one category — unify them; also watch units, kilograms versus pounds — the Mars Climate Orbiter was lost to exactly that mistake, three hundred million dollars. Six — validate: re-run info and describe, confirm the holes are filled, ranges are sane, and document every change you made in a cleaning log. That last habit — write down what you did to the data — is what separates a professional from a tinkerer: in six months, you won't remember, but your log will.

六步，按顺序。一——检查：刚才做过了；info、isnull-sum、describe——知己知彼。二——处理缺失值：整个下一页讲决策路径。三——处理异常值：先检测再决策——三法三选，再下一页。四——修类型与去重：日期列被读成字符串？pd.to_datetime。数值列混进文本？强制转换后重新检查。重复行：df.duplicated().sum() 再 df.drop_duplicates()。五——统一格式："M"/"male"/"Male" 是一个类别的三种写法——统一；还要盯单位，千克对磅——火星气候探测器就是栽在这一处，三亿美元。六——验证：重跑 info 和 describe，确认洞已补、范围合理，并把每一步改动写进清洗日志。最后这个习惯——把你对数据做过什么写下来——是专业者与折腾者的分界线：六个月后你自己会忘，但日志记得。

p20缺失值决策 · Missing DataTiers by Ratio + 4 Fill Strategies~4.5 min

Missing values are not a bug to stamp out — they're a decision to make, and the first question is always: how much is missing? Three tiers. Under 5%: the column is basically healthy — fill or drop with a clear conscience. 5% to 30%: filling starts to distort — the choice matters, think before you act. Age at 20% sits right in this band. Over 30%: like Cabin at 77% — the column is mostly vacuum; filling it means inventing data. Standard move: drop the column, or convert it into a signal — "was cabin recorded: yes/no" — because sometimes missingness itself carries meaning: maybe cabin numbers were recorded mainly for first-class survivors. Then, four fill strategies, in rising order of sophistication. Drop: df.dropna(subset=["Embarked"]) — two rows out of 891, safe. Fill with constant: unknown category. Fill with statistic: Age gets the median — median, not mean, because Fare's right tail drags means but leaves medians untouched. Model-based fill: predict Age from other columns — that's Week 3 material, just know it exists. The one thing you must never do: fill with zero or -1 and forget about it — you'd tell the model that five-month-old babies and the unrecorded are the same thing.

缺失值不是要消灭的 bug，而是要做的决策，第一个问题永远是：缺了多少？三档。5% 以下：这列基本健康——放心填或放心删。5% 到 30%：填充开始失真——选择有讲究，先想后动；Age 的 20% 正落在这档。30% 以上：像 Cabin 的 77%——这列基本是真空，填它等于编造数据。标准动作：删列，或转成信号——"舱位是否被记录：是/否"——因为有时缺失本身有含义：也许舱号主要记录的是头等舱生还者。然后四种填充策略，按精巧程度递增。删除：df.dropna(subset=["Embarked"])——891 里删 2 行，安全。常数填充：unknown 类别。统计量填充：Age 用中位数——中位数而非均值，因为 Fare 的右尾会拖动均值，却动不了中位数。模型填充：用其他列预测 Age——第 3 周的内容，知道存在即可。绝对不能做的一件事：填 0 或 -1 然后忘了——那是告诉模型，五个月大的婴儿和没登记的人是同一种东西。

提问 ASK"Cabin 缺 77%，你会怎么处理？举手选：删列 / 填常数 / 缺失当信号"（大多数场景选删列或转信号）
p21异常值 · OutliersIQR / Z-score / Domain Rules + 3 Dispositions~4.5 min

An outlier is a point that lives far from the crowd. First — detection, three methods. IQR: sort, take the 25th and 75th percentiles, compute the interquartile range, and flag anything below Q1 minus 1.5 IQR or above Q3 plus 1.5 IQR. Robust — one crazy value can't move the quartiles. This is your default. Z-score: how many standard deviations from the mean; flag beyond 3. Cleaner math, but the mean and std are themselves dragged by outliers — the detector is infected by what it detects, so use it only for roughly normal data. Domain rules: the expert's eye — a human Age of 200 is impossible regardless of statistics; a Fare of 0 might be crew, babies, or a data entry blank. Then — and this is the part students forget — detection is not deletion. Three dispositions: keep, because it's real — that 512-pound fare was a genuine luxury ticket, deleting it would erase the truth; fix, when there's evidence of an error — a misplaced decimal, a unit slip; remove, only when it's provably wrong and would poison the model. My rule: every removal gets a reason written in the log, and "it looked weird" is not a reason.

异常值是离群索居的点。先说检测，三种方法。IQR：排序，取 25% 和 75% 分位数，算四分位距，低于 Q1−1.5·IQR 或高于 Q3+1.5·IQR 的标记出来。稳健——一个疯值拖不动分位数，这是你的默认选择。Z-score：偏离均值几个标准差，超过 3 标记。数学更干净，但均值和标准差本身就会被异常值拖走——探测器被探测对象感染——只用于近似正态的数据。领域规则：专家的眼睛——人类 Age=200 无论统计上多"正常"都不可能；Fare=0 可能是船员、婴儿，也可能是录入空格。然后——学生最容易忘的部分——检测不等于删除。三种处置：保留，因为它是真实的——512 英镑的票价是真实存在的奢华票，删了它等于抹掉真相；修正，有证据表明是错误时——小数点错位、单位搞混；删除，仅当可证明是错的且会毒害模型。我的规矩：每一次删除都要在日志里写明理由，"看着不顺眼"不算理由。

p22GIGO · 垃圾进，垃圾出Hero · Garbage In, Garbage Out~3 min

Let this dark slide burn itself into your memory — it's the motto of everything we did today. Garbage in, garbage out. The model is a mirror, not a magician: it reflects the data you feed it. Feed it clean, representative data, and it learns real patterns. Feed it noise, bias, or holes, and it will confidently, efficiently, at scale — produce garbage. No algorithm rescues bad data; that's not an opinion, that's the architecture of machine learning itself: the model can only be as good as its training distribution. So when your model fails in the future — and it will — before you blame the algorithm, before you tune hyperparameters at midnight, go back and interrogate the data first. Nine times out of ten, the bug is not in the model. The bug is in the pipeline that fed it. That's why this week exists, and that's why Week 3 will feel easy by comparison: the person who cleaned the data well has already won half the modeling battle.

让这页深色幻灯片烧进你的记忆——它是今天所有内容的主旋律。垃圾进，垃圾出。模型是镜子，不是魔法师：它照出你喂给它的数据。喂干净、有代表性的数据，它学到真实模式；喂噪声、偏见和空洞，它会自信地、高效地、规模化地——产出垃圾。没有任何算法能拯救坏数据——这不是观点，是机器学习的架构本身：模型的上限就是它训练分布的上限。所以将来你的模型失败时——一定会有那么一天——在你怪算法之前、在半夜调超参数之前，先回去审问数据。十次里有九次，bug 不在模型里，而在喂它的管线里。这就是为什么有这一周，也是为什么第 3 周相比之下会显得轻松：把数据洗干净的人，建模战役已经赢了一半。
