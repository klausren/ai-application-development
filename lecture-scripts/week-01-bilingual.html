p7扉页 02 · AI 是什么Part 02 Divider~0.5 min

Part two. Four words you hear every day — AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI. Most people use them interchangeably. By the end of the next slide, you won't.

第二部分。AI、机器学习、深度学习、生成式 AI——四个你每天都听到的词，多数人混着用。下一页之后，你就不会了。

p8AI 层级关系 · The HierarchyAI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL ⊃ GenAI~7 min

This nested diagram is, in my opinion, the single most important picture of the whole course. From outside in: Artificial Intelligence is the big circle — any technique that makes machines behave intelligently, including rule-based systems from the 1980s. Inside it, Machine Learning — systems that learn patterns from data instead of being explicitly programmed. Inside that, Deep Learning — machine learning that uses multi-layer neural networks to learn features automatically. And at the center, Generative AI — deep learning that creates new content: text, images, code. So when someone says "deep learning is not machine learning" — you now know that's wrong; deep learning is a subset. Here is your practical tool: the three questions. One — does the system learn from data? If no, it's classic AI. Two — does it use multi-layer neural networks? If yes, it's deep learning. Three — does it generate new content? If yes, it's generative AI. Let's practice: ChatGPT? Learns from data — yes. Deep networks — yes. Generates content — yes. So it's generative AI. A spam filter from 2005? Learns from data — yes. Deep networks — no. So it's classic machine learning. Your turn: a chess engine that searches millions of positions per second but never learns — which circle?

这张嵌套图我认为是全课程最重要的一张图。从外到内：人工智能是大圈——让机器表现智能的任何技术，包括上世纪 80 年代的规则系统；里面的机器学习——从数据中学习规律而非被显式编程；再里面的深度学习——用多层神经网络自动学特征的机器学习；最中心的生成式 AI——创造新内容的深度学习：文本、图像、代码。所以"深度学习不是机器学习"这种说法是错的，它是子集。给你一个实用工具——判断三连问：①系统从数据中学习吗？不学就是经典 AI。②用多层神经网络吗？用了就是深度学习。③生成新内容吗？生成就是生成式 AI。练一下：ChatGPT？从数据学——是；深网络——是；生成内容——是，所以是生成式 AI。2005 年的垃圾邮件过滤器？从数据学——是；深网络——否，所以是经典机器学习。轮到你：一个每秒搜索数百万棋位但从不学习的国际象棋引擎——在哪个圈？

p9机器学习三大范式 · Three ParadigmsSupervised / Unsupervised / RL~6 min

Machine learning itself comes in three flavors, and the difference is beautifully simple — it's about what kind of feedback the data gives you. Supervised learning: the data comes with answers. Every email is labeled "spam" or "not spam" — the algorithm learns the mapping from input to answer. That's classification and regression, and it is the focus of this course. Unsupervised learning: no answers at all, just raw data, and the algorithm finds hidden structure — customer segmentation, anomaly detection. Reinforcement learning: no answers, but rewards. An agent tries things, gets points or penalties, and slowly learns a strategy — that's how AlphaGo learned to play Go. Now the quick check at the bottom: you have 100,000 customers, no labels, and you want to group them into five segments by purchasing behavior — which paradigm? Right — no labels, looking for structure — that's unsupervised learning, clustering. We will do this hands-on in Week 3.

机器学习本身分三种范式，区别简单得漂亮——取决于数据给你什么样的反馈。监督学习：数据带答案。每封邮件都标注了"垃圾/非垃圾"，算法学习输入到答案的映射——就是分类与回归，也是本课程的重点。无监督学习：完全没有答案，只有原始数据，算法自己找隐藏结构——用户分群、异常检测。强化学习：没有答案但有奖励。智能体不断尝试、得分或扣分，慢慢学出策略——AlphaGo 就是这么学会下围棋的。底部快问快答：10 万个用户、没有任何标签、想按消费行为分成 5 群——哪种范式？对——无标签、找结构——无监督学习（聚类）。第 3 周我们会动手做。

p10三次发展浪潮 · Three WavesSymbolic → Statistical → Deep~5 min

A quick history in one timeline — three waves. The first wave, symbolic AI: humans hand-wrote rules, "if this then that". It produced expert systems — impressive for narrow tasks, but brittle: every situation needed a new rule, and humans had to know the answer in advance. The second wave, statistical learning: instead of writing rules, we feed data to statistical models and let them find the rules — this powered spam filters and search engines, roughly the 1990s to 2000s. The third wave, deep learning: massive data plus massive compute plus neural networks — and since 2022, the generative explosion. Here is why this matters to you: we are living in the application-engineering window of the third wave. The models exist, the APIs exist, the tooling exists — what the industry lacks is people who can turn them into reliable products. That's you, in three years.

一条时间轴讲完三次浪潮。第一波符号主义：人工手写规则、"如果……那么……"，产出了专家系统——窄任务上惊艳但脆弱：每种情况都要新规则，而且人必须提前知道答案。第二波统计学习：不写规则了，把数据喂给统计模型让它自己找规律——垃圾邮件过滤、搜索引擎，大约 1990-2000 年代。第三波深度学习：海量数据+海量算力+神经网络，2022 年后进入生成式爆发。为什么这与你有关：我们正处在第三波的应用工程化窗口期——模型有了、API 有了、工具链有了，行业缺的是能把它们变成可靠产品的人。三年后，那就是你。

p11AI 寒冬 · The AI Winters1970s / 1990s~3 min

But this history is not a straight line — it has two deep valleys, the so-called AI winters. Around the mid-1970s, funding collapsed when symbolic AI promised too much and delivered too little — machines could play chess but couldn't understand everyday language. And again in the early 1990s, expert systems proved too expensive to maintain, and the field went quiet for a decade. The lesson I want you to take: AI progress alternates between hype cycles and winters, and the gap between promise and delivery is what causes the fall. When you hear "AI will solve everything next year", remember the winters. And when you hear "AI is a bubble that will burst", remember AlexNet, 2012. The truth is in between, and engineers who can tell the difference are the ones who get hired.

但这段历史不是直线——它有两道深谷，即所谓AI 寒冬。1970 年代中期，符号主义承诺过多、兑现太少，资金崩塌——机器会下棋却听不懂日常语言。1990 年代初专家系统维护成本过高，整个领域沉寂了十年。我想让你带走的是：AI 的进步在 hype 周期与寒冬之间交替，而"承诺与兑现的落差"正是坠落的原因。当你听到"AI 明年解决一切"，想想寒冬；听到"AI 是要破裂的泡沫"，想想 2012 年的 AlexNet。真相在两者之间，而能分辨的人才有人要。

p12为什么是软件工程师 · Why SE Students3 Structural Shifts~3 min

So why is this course in a software engineering program, not a math program? Because three structural shifts happened in this industry. First: feature engineering is becoming data engineering — the scarce skill is no longer hand-crafting clever features but building reliable data pipelines. Second: writing rules is becoming tuning systems — you no longer encode business logic by hand; you configure, fine-tune and evaluate learned systems. Third: single applications are becoming agent ecosystems — today's AI products are systems of models, APIs, memory and tools working together. Notice that all three shifts are engineering problems, not research problems. Your leverage as an SE student is turning models into products. The researchers will keep making the models smarter. The market pays for the people who make them usable.

那为什么这门课开在软件工程专业而不是数学系？因为这个行业发生了三个结构性转变。第一：特征工程正在变成数据工程——稀缺技能不再是手搓精巧特征，而是构建可靠的数据管道。第二：写规则正在变成调系统——业务逻辑不再靠手写编码，而是配置、微调与评估学习型系统。第三：单应用正在变成智能体生态——今天的 AI 产品是模型、API、记忆与工具协同的系统。注意：这三个转变全是工程问题，不是研究问题。软件工程学生的杠杆，就是把模型变成产品。研究者会让模型更聪明，而市场为"让模型可用"的人付钱。

p14四大应用域 · Four DomainsCV / NLP / Speech / RecSys~5 min

Four application domains dominate the industry. Computer vision — machines that see: defect inspection, face recognition, medical imaging. Natural language processing — machines that read and write: translation, chatbots, code assistants like the one you'll build in Module 3. Speech and multimodal — machines that listen and speak: voice assistants, and today's models that handle text, image and audio together. And recommendation and decision systems — machines that choose: what video you see next, what route your delivery takes. Here is the unifying insight: all four domains reduce to the same pipeline — perceive, understand, generate, decide. Keep that in mind, because your Capstone will be built on exactly this pattern: perceive something, understand it, and act on it.

四个应用域统治着行业。计算机视觉——会看的机器：缺陷检测、人脸识别、医学影像。自然语言处理——会读写：翻译、聊天机器人、代码助手，你会在模块三亲手做一个。语音与多模态——会听会说：语音助手，以及今天同时处理文本图像音频的模型。推荐与决策系统——会选择：下一个视频看什么、快递走哪条路。统一性的洞见是：四个域最终都归结为同一条流水线——感知、理解、生成、决策。记住它，你的 Capstone 就建立在这个模式上：感知某物、理解它、对它采取行动。

p15六大突破 · Six Breakthroughs2012–2023+~5 min

Six moments changed everything. 2012, AlexNet: a deep network crushed the ImageNet competition, error rate dropped ten points overnight, and GPU training became the standard — that's the ignition of deep learning. 2016, AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol, and the whole world suddenly paid attention. 2017, Transformer — one paper, "Attention Is All You Need", became the foundation of every large model you use today. 2020, GPT-3: 175 billion parameters, and for the first time, scale alone produced new abilities. 2022, ChatGPT: one hundred million users in two months — generative AI went mainstream. 2023 and beyond: multimodal models that understand text, image, audio and video together. Now, how to read this timeline: three observations. The pace is accelerating — AlexNet to ChatGPT took ten years, now every year is a milestone. The paradigms are converging — vision, language, speech all converged on the Transformer. And the entry barrier is dropping — paper-to-product used to take years, now months. That last one is your window.

六个时刻改变了一切。2012 AlexNet：深网络横扫 ImageNet，错误率一夜降十个百分点，GPU 训练成为标配——深度学习的引爆点。2016 AlphaGo 击败李世石，全世界开始关注。2017 Transformer——一篇《Attention Is All You Need》成为你今天用的所有大模型的底座。2020 GPT-3：1750 亿参数，第一次"规模本身"带来新能力。2022 ChatGPT：两个月一亿用户——生成式 AI 进入主流。2023+ 多模态：文本、图像、音频、视频统一理解。这条时间轴怎么读？三个观察：节奏在加快——AlexNet 到 ChatGPT 用了十年，现在每年都是里程碑；范式在收敛——视觉、语言、语音都收敛到 Transformer；门槛在降低——从论文到产品从数年缩到数月。最后一条就是你的窗口。

p16职业地图 · Career Roles4 Roles × Course Modules~4 min

Let me connect this to your future salary. Four roles in an AI team. The ML researcher invents new architectures — that path usually needs a PhD; honestly, it's not the majority path. The data engineer builds the pipelines that keep data flowing — critical, and covered by our Week 2 focus. The AI application engineer takes trained models and turns them into products — APIs, interfaces, integrations. And MLOps keeps it all running in production — monitoring, retraining, deployment. Look at the mapping to our modules: we deliberately train you across all four roles, but with a center of gravity on the last two. For SE students, the highest-leverage careers are AI application engineer and MLOps — because that's where the industry bottleneck is right now, and where your engineering training is a direct advantage.

把这些和你未来的薪水连起来。AI 团队里有四类角色：ML 研究员发明新架构——这条路通常需要 PhD，坦白说不是多数人的路；数据工程师构建数据流动的管道——关键，对应我们第 2 周的重点；AI 应用工程师把训练好的模型变成产品——API、界面、集成；MLOps让一切在生产环境跑起来——监控、重训、部署。看它们与课程模块的对应：我们刻意让你四种角色都接触，但重心在后两个。对软件工程学生，杠杆最高的就是 AI 应用工程师与 MLOps——因为那正是当前行业瓶颈，也是你工程训练的直接优势所在。

p17AI 伦理与工程师责任 · Ethics & ResponsibilityBias / Privacy / Hallucination / Accountability~5 min

Last page of this part — the one I take most seriously. Four risks will appear again and again in your career. Bias: training data carries historical prejudice, and models learn it and amplify it — Amazon's recruiting tool downgraded women's resumes, and they had to abandon it in 2018. Privacy: models can memorize and leak personal data from their training set — a chatbot once recited real phone numbers. Hallucination: generative models produce fluent, confident, completely fabricated facts — AI-cited papers that don't exist, deepfake fraud. Accountability: when an AI system fails, who is responsible — the developer, the deployer, or the user? The autonomous-driving lawsuits are still writing the answer. Now read the blue box with me: in this course, every deliverable includes an ethics note — two sentences are enough, but you must answer one question: "who could this system harm, and what did I do about it?" Because when you graduate, that question will be in your code review, whether you like it or not.

p18扉页 04 · 项目生命周期Part 04 Divider~0.5 min

Part four is the skeleton of everything you will do in this course: the lifecycle of an AI project.

第四部分是你在本课程要做的一切事情的骨架：AI 项目的生命周期。

p19六阶段框架 · The Six StagesDefine → Data → Features → Model → Deploy → Monitor~5 min

Every AI project, from a weekend hackathon to a national-scale system, walks through six stages. One, problem definition — what exactly are we predicting, and is AI even the right tool? Two, data collection — where does the data come from, is it legal to use, is it enough? Three, feature engineering — turning raw data into signals a model can learn from. Four, modeling — choosing, training and comparing models; notice this is only one stage out of six. Five, deployment — wrapping the model behind an API or an app so real users can touch it. Six, monitoring — watching performance in production, because data drifts and models decay. Memorize this chain — your Capstone will be graded against all six stages, and your midterm proposal must cover the first three.

每个 AI 项目——从周末黑客松到国家级系统——都要走六个阶段。①问题定义：到底预测什么？AI 是不是对的工具？②数据收集：数据从哪来、用了合法吗、够不够？③特征工程：把原始数据变成模型能学的信号。④建模：选模型、训练、对比——注意这只是六阶段之一。⑤部署：把模型包进 API 或应用，让真实用户摸得到。⑥监控：盯着生产环境的表现，因为数据会漂移、模型会衰减。背下这条链——你的 Capstone 会按这六阶段逐段评分，期中开题必须覆盖前三阶段。

提示 TIP在板书写六阶段箭头链，后面案例页反复回指
p20案例走查：食堂排队预测 · Case WalkthroughCanteen Queue Prediction~9 min

Let's make it real with one example carried through all six stages: predicting the canteen queue time. Stage one, definition: we want to predict, for each food window, the queue length in the next fifteen minutes, within a five-minute error. And here's a modeling decision hidden inside the definition — is this classification or regression? Queue time in minutes — that's a number, so regression. Stage two, data: POS machine transactions — what dishes, at what time; camera-based people counting; and the university calendar, because exam weeks change everything. And note the pitfall: camera data must be anonymized — no face storage. That's stage-two ethics, not an afterthought. Stage three, features: time of day, day of week, weather, menu popularity, foot traffic in the previous fifteen minutes. Common-sense features often beat fancy models. Stage four, modeling: always run a mean baseline first — just predicting the average queue time — then a gradient-boosted tree, and compare. If you skip the baseline, you're flying blind. Stage five, deployment: wrap it in a REST API with FastAPI, and the mini-program pulls a fresh prediction every five minutes. The model is not the product — the interface is. Stage six, monitoring: a weekly error report; and when a new canteen opens, the data distribution shifts — that's drift, and it triggers retraining. The day you deploy is the day the project really begins.

用一个贯穿六阶段的例子把它变真实：预测食堂排队时长。阶段一定义：预测每个窗口未来 15 分钟的排队时长，误差 ±5 分钟内。定义里藏着一个建模决策——这是分类还是回归？排队分钟数是数字，所以是回归。阶段二数据：POS 机流水（什么菜、什么时间）、摄像头人流计数、校历（考试周改变一切）。注意坑：摄像头数据必须脱敏——不能存人脸，这是阶段二的伦理，不是事后补丁。阶段三特征：时段、星期、天气、菜单热度、前 15 分钟人流量。常识特征常常打败花哨模型。阶段四建模：永远先跑均值基线——就预测平均排队时长——再上梯度提升树做对比。跳过基线等于裸奔。阶段五部署：FastAPI 封装 REST 接口，小程序每 5 分钟拉一次新预测。模型不是产品，接口才是。阶段六监控：预测误差周报；新食堂开业意味着数据分布变了——这就是漂移，触发重训。上线那天，项目才真正开始。

提问 ASK在"分类还是回归"处停下让学生投票再公布提示 TIP本页 9 分钟是理论段最长单页，配合板书六阶段链回指
p2180% 法则 · The 80% RuleHero · Data & Engineering~5 min

Here is a number that might surprise you: across real AI projects, engineers spend roughly eighty percent of their time on data and engineering — collecting it, cleaning it, building pipelines, deploying, monitoring — and only about twenty percent on the modeling itself. Training the model is the fun part, yes, but it's the small part. This single number explains two things. It explains this course's design: why we spend all of Week 2 on data, why labs are seventy percent of your grade, why Module 4 is entirely about engineering. And it explains your career advantage: if you only know how to call model APIs, you compete with everyone. If you can own the other eighty percent — the pipelines, the deployment, the monitoring — you become the person the team cannot ship without.

一个可能让你意外的数字：真实 AI 项目中，工程师大约80% 的时间花在数据与工程上——收集、清洗、建管道、部署、监控——建模本身只占约 20%。训练模型当然有趣，但那是小头。这个数字解释了两件事。它解释这门课的设计：为什么第 2 周整个星期讲数据、为什么实验占成绩 70%、为什么模块四全讲工程化。它也解释你的职业优势：只会调模型 API，你在和所有人竞争；而能拿下另外 80%——管道、部署、监控——你就是团队发版离不开的那个人。
