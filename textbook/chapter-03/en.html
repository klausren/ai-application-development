Chapter 03 · Module 1 · Week 3

Data Preparation and Pipelines

Chapter 2 built a model out of raw tensors. This chapter deliberately turns away from the model and looks at what feeds it. The claim is blunt: data sets the ceiling and models only approach it. You will build the six-step pipeline — raw data, cleaning, splitting, Dataset, DataLoader, batches — in PyTorch, learn to detect and avoid the four shapes of data leakage, handle augmentation and class imbalance without fooling yourself, and finish by writing a Data Card that states where your conclusions stop being valid.

3.1 Data Sets the Ceiling

Every project that stalls has the same first question: should we improve the model or the data? Chapter 2 showed that parameters are learned from data, which already hints at the answer. This chapter makes it explicit.

Thesis The information and label quality in your dataset determine how low the error can possibly go. A model can only approach that limit. If the ceiling is low, no architecture rescues it — and if the split is wrong, you cannot even tell what the ceiling is.

3.1.1 Three Consequences

1 · The ceiling is real

How much of the world your data covers, and how accurately it is labelled, bound the achievable error. A feature that was never recorded cannot be recovered by a bigger network.

2 · A better model only approaches it

Swapping architectures and tuning hyper-parameters moves you along the same ceiling. When progress stops, the next useful action is almost always an inspection of the data.

3 · Measurement decides whether you know

A wrong split or a leak produces a number that is not merely optimistic — it is meaningless. Everything later in this chapter serves one goal: seeing the true level.

3.1.2 The Six-Step Pipeline

Raw data — many sources and formats, not yet good or bad.
Clean — missing values, outliers, duplicates and inconsistent units, each handled with an explicit criterion.
Split — train / validation / test, with a fixed seed and a strategy you can justify.
Dataset — wraps the data so that one sample is addressable: __len__ and __getitem__.
DataLoader — batching, shuffling, parallel loading and batch assembly.
Batches — what the training loop actually consumes.
The Order Is Not Negotiable Splitting must happen before any step that learns from data — scaling, imputation, feature selection and augmentation all count. Every statistic computed over the whole dataset is a statistic your validation and test rows contributed to.

3.1.3 Model-Centric vs Data-Centric

Model-centricData-centric
Starting moveFeed the data to a model as it isDiagnose quality; settle provenance and compliance
Main leverSwap architectures, tune hyper-parametersFix splits, labels, duplicates, coverage
Typical outcomeGains shrink and cannot be explainedThe same model gives steadier, higher metrics
When it stallsYou cannot say whyErrors are traceable to a concrete data problem

The two are not rivals; they are ordered. Do the data first, then the model. In the opposite order you cannot even judge whether the model is good.

3.2 Acquisition, Cleaning and Provenance

Cleaning is not "making the data look nice". It is turning known problems into an auditable record: for every problem, a criterion that detects it, an action that addresses it, and a note on what the action can break.

3.2.1 Four Common Problems

Missing values

Rank the missing rates, then check whether missingness relates to the label. Imputation statistics (median, mode) may come from the training set only.

Outliers

Judge with the 1/99 percentiles or the IQR rule, not by "this looks strange". You may clip, transform, or keep them with a flag.

Duplicates & inconsistency

De-duplicate on named keys; unify units and category codes. A duplicate spanning two splits is direct leakage.

Label noise

Cross-check, measure annotator agreement, spot-check by hand. A high noise rate cannot be compensated with more data.

ProblemHow you detect itActionRisk
Missingdf.isna().mean() ranked; missingness cross-tabulated with the labelDrop, median/mode imputation, model-based imputationImputing with full-data statistics is leakage
OutliersPercentiles, box plots, the IQR ruleClip, log-transform, or keep with a flagThe outlier may be exactly what you must predict
DuplicatesKey counts; checks on unit and code value setsDe-duplicate; unify units and codesOne entity across train and test means leakage
Label noiseAgreement warnings, high-confidence errors, manual samplingRe-label, remove, or use a robust lossQuantify the noise rate before deciding to clean

3.2.2 Provenance and Privacy Come First

Before the first line of cleaning code, answer two questions: where did this data come from, and under what permission? and does it contain personal information? Undocumented provenance makes a result unreproducible and, in many jurisdictions, unlawful to publish. Personal data must be de-identified before it enters the pipeline, and raw data must never be pasted into a public service. Respecting the source of your data is not a formality — it is the baseline of engineering ethics.

3.2.3 Using AI to Explore Without Being Misled

Asking an AI to draft an exploration block is an efficient use of the tool. The discipline is that every statistical claim it produces must be reproducible by you with one assert or one plot:

import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("data/raw/defects.csv")
print("shape:", df.shape)
print(df["label"].value_counts(normalize=True).round(3))

# An AI will happily say: "classes are balanced, train as is."
# Settle it instead of believing it:
share = df["label"].value_counts(normalize=True)
assert share.min() > 0.20, "minority class is too small for 'balanced'"

# "There are no missing values."
assert df.isna().sum().sum() == 0, "there are missing values"

# "Features correlate strongly with the label." -> plot it and recompute
df.groupby("label")["size"].mean().plot(kind="bar")
Rule When an AI's statistic disagrees with a plot you produced, trust what you ran. Record the disagreement, the prompt and the corrected conclusion in AI_USE.md. That log is assessed — it is evidence of verification, not a penalty.

3.3 Splitting: Ratios and Strategies

The split decides whether every number you see afterwards is trustworthy. It is the single most consequential decision in this chapter.

3.3.1 What Each Split Is Allowed to Do

SplitShareJobHow often you may use it
Training~70%Fit parameters and all statistics: scaling, imputation, feature selection, augmentationConstantly
Validation~15%Choose models and tune hyper-parametersRepeatedly — so it is not the final score
Test~15%Final estimate of real-world performanceOnce
Iron Rule Once the test set informs tuning, it has silently become a validation set and the reported number stops meaning anything. When data is scarce (under about 10,000 rows), use 5-fold cross-validation on the training portion instead of a fixed validation set.

3.3.2 Four Strategies — Chosen by the Nature of the Data

StrategyUse whenFails when
RandomSamples are independent, with no time order and no repeated entityAny of the three conditions is violated
StratifiedClasses are imbalanced, especially with few minority samplesYou need entity-level separation instead of class-level
Time-basedThere is a real order: time series, streams, logs—
GroupOne user, device, patient or document has many records—

A random split on a time series lets the model see the future during training. A random split when one device contributes hundreds of rows puts near-duplicates on both sides of the line — that is leakage, even though no code is wrong.

3.3.3 A Seeded, Reproducible Split in PyTorch

import torch
from torch.utils.data import Subset

# A torch.Generator keeps the split reproducible without touching global RNG state
g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(42)
perm = torch.randperm(len(ds), generator=g)

i1, i2 = int(0.70 * len(ds)), int(0.85 * len(ds))
train_ds = Subset(ds, perm[:i1])
val_ds   = Subset(ds, perm[i1:i2])
test_ds  = Subset(ds, perm[i2:])

print(len(train_ds), len(val_ds), len(test_ds))

For imbalanced classes, do the permutation within each class and concatenate the parts. The three splits then keep the overall class ratio, and a minority class does not vanish from the validation set by chance.

3.4 Data Leakage

Leakage means the model is trained on information that will not be available at inference time. The score it produces is not merely optimistic; it does not correspond to any real deployment.

3.4.1 Four Shapes of Leakage

ShapeWhat it looks like in codeWhy it inflates the score
Test rows in trainingSplitting, then concatenating the test set back into the training dataThe model has seen the exam it is being graded on
Duplicates across splitsA de-duplication step that never ran, so one entity appears in two splitsNear-identical rows make recall trivial
Random split on ordered datarandom_split on a time seriesThe model interpolates between past and future
Statistics from the full datasetStandardScaler-style scaling or imputation before the splitValidation and test rows contribute to the mean the model learns from

3.4.2 The One Question That Catches All Four

Test Could I obtain this information at inference time? If not, it must not appear in training. Ask it of every column, every statistic and every preprocessing step.

3.4.3 One Wrong Way, One Right Way

from torch.utils.data import random_split

# WRONG - statistics fitted over everything, then split
mu = X.mean(0)
X_norm = (X - mu) / X.std(0)
train_ds, val_ds, test_ds = random_split(ds, [0.7, 0.15, 0.15])

# RIGHT - split first, statistics from the training rows only
g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(42)
train_ds, val_ds, test_ds = random_split(
    ds, [0.70, 0.15, 0.15], generator=g)

tr = X[train_ds.indices]                 # training rows only
mu, sd = tr.mean(0), tr.std(0).clamp_min(1e-8)
X_norm = (X - mu) / sd                   # apply the same transform to all

A real incident makes the cost concrete: a team trained on a frame that still contained the test samples, reported 0.99 offline, and dropped to 0.78 in production. The 21 points were not learning — the model had seen the exam.

3.5 Dataset and DataLoader

After splitting, the data must reach the training loop as indexable samples. PyTorch does this with two abstractions: a Dataset defines what one sample is; a DataLoader defines how batches are formed.

3.5.1 The Dataset Contract

import torch
from torch.utils.data import Dataset

class DefectDataset(Dataset):
    """DataFrame -> PyTorch dataset: two methods only."""

    def __init__(self, df, transform=None):
        self.y = torch.tensor(df["label"].values, dtype=torch.long)
        self.x = torch.tensor(
            df.drop(columns=["label"]).values.astype("float32"))
        self.transform = transform       # training split only

    def __len__(self):
        return len(self.y)             # the batch count is derived from this

    def __getitem__(self, i):
        x = self.x[i]                    # exactly one sample
        if self.transform is not None:
            x = self.transform(x)
        return x, self.y[i]              # (features, label)

ds = DefectDataset(df)
print(len(ds), ds[0][0].shape)     # 12480 torch.Size([12])
Two Disciplines Never compute cross-sample statistics inside __getitem__ — a column mean or a global vocabulary there is fitted over the whole dataset and is a breeding ground for leakage. And pass transform to the training split only: validation and test must match inference.

3.5.2 The Four Jobs of a DataLoader

from torch.utils.data import DataLoader

train_loader = DataLoader(train_ds, batch_size=32, shuffle=True,
                          num_workers=4, drop_last=True)
val_loader   = DataLoader(val_ds,   batch_size=64, shuffle=False)
test_loader  = DataLoader(test_ds,  batch_size=64, shuffle=False)

xb, yb = next(iter(train_loader))
print(xb.shape, yb.shape, len(train_loader))   # (32, 12) (32,) 273

3.6 Augmentation and Class Imbalance

Two boundaries govern this section. Augmentation touches the training set only. Any sampling step must report the class distribution before and after it ran.

3.6.1 Augmentation Is Training-Only

Images: flip, crop, colour jitter, random erasing. Tabular data: small Gaussian noise, interpolation, feature perturbation. The transform must not change the label semantics — otherwise you are manufacturing noise with a label on it.

3.6.2 Handling Imbalance: Two Options

import torch
from torch.utils.data import DataLoader, WeightedRandomSampler

# option 1 - resample: the rarer class gets a larger weight
y_tr = torch.tensor([ds[i][1] for i in train_ds.indices])
counts = torch.bincount(y_tr)
w = (1.0 / counts.float())[y_tr]
sampler = WeightedRandomSampler(weights=w, num_samples=len(w),
                                replacement=True)
train_loader = DataLoader(train_ds, batch_size=32, sampler=sampler)
# note: sampler and shuffle=True are mutually exclusive

# option 2 - cost-sensitive loss: the data is untouched
import torch.nn as nn
criterion = nn.CrossEntropyLoss(weight=1.0 / counts.float())

3.6.3 Under Imbalance, Accuracy Is the Wrong Metric

If one class holds 4% of the data, a model that always predicts the majority class reads 0.96 accuracy and has learned nothing. Switch to per-class recall, F1, or AUPRC, and report the class distribution before and after the treatment. Every treatment is a trade-off: if minority recall rises while the majority class collapses, you have paid overall performance for minority performance, and that must be stated rather than hidden.

3.7 Pitfalls and a Debugging Checklist

Checklist
  • Split first. Every step that learns — scaling, imputation, feature selection, augmentation — comes after the split and touches the training set only.
  • Statistics come from train. Means, variances, quantiles and vocabularies are computed on the training rows and then applied to val and test.
  • One entity, one split. All records of a user, device, patient or document stay together.
  • Ordered data gets a time-based split. Shuffling lets the model see the future.
  • The test set is not reused. Once it selects a model or a hyper-parameter, it is a validation set.
  • Fix the seeds. Use torch.Generator for the split and for shuffle, and worker_init_fn for per-worker seeds.
  • Check the batch shape first. xb must be (B, F); if not, look at __getitem__.
  • Never augment val/test. Their numbers must be comparable to inference.
  • Report the distribution before and after sampling. Otherwise the bias you introduced is invisible.
  • Keep the Data Card current. A pipeline whose biases are undocumented is not finished.

3.8 Summary and Exercises

Five Takeaways
  1. Data sets the ceiling; a model only approaches it. When results are poor, diagnose the data before changing the architecture.
  2. Cleaning turns known problems into an auditable record: missing, outliers, duplicates and label noise each get a criterion, an action and a stated risk.
  3. Splitting precedes every step that learns; the statistics behind scaling, imputation and feature selection come from the training set only.
  4. Leakage means training on information unavailable at inference. Test the pipeline with one question: could I obtain this at inference time?
  5. A Dataset defines one sample and a DataLoader defines the batches; augmentation is training-only, and imbalance is treated by a sampler or a cost-sensitive loss and re-measured with per-class metrics.
